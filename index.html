SUNSET

The journey begins.

When the sun sets in the west, Ra begins his nighttime journey. He leaves the sky and enters the Duat, the Egyptian underworld. The sunset represents the end of the day and the beginning of Ra’s journey through darkness.

Key idea: Sunset marks the start of Ra’s journey through the night.

FINAL MAJOR ARTWORK

Our Final Artwork

Here is our final major artwork for the Journey of Ra.

Our Reflection

At first, we saw the Journey of Ra as simply a story about the sun traveling through the underworld. But while working on the artwork, we started to see it more as a cycle of struggle and renewal. Ra is surrounded by a bright sunset, but underneath him is Apophis and the darkness of the Duat. For us, that contrast shows that even when something looks peaceful, there can still be challenges ahead.

What we like about the created artwork is that it does not only show Ra as a god, but also shows the journey he has to go through every night before the sun can rise again. We think this makes the story more relatable because people also go through difficult moments before reaching a new beginning. The artwork made us appreciate how mythology can express ideas about life, struggle, hope, and starting again through visual elements instead of words alone.

ART APPRECIATION

Why we designed it this way.

We designed the artwork to show the contrast between Ra's light and the darkness of the Duat. Instead of making every part highly detailed, we focused on the important parts of the story: Ra, the solar boat, the sun, the Egyptian landscape, and Apophis. We wanted someone looking at the artwork to understand that Ra is moving from the world of light toward something darker and more dangerous.

The composition also places Ra near the center because he is the main focus of the journey, while Apophis takes up much of the bottom of the artwork to make the danger feel large and unavoidable. At the same time, the bright sun behind Ra reminds the viewer that the journey is still connected to life, light, and rebirth.

COLOR

Light against darkness

We used warm colors such as orange, yellow, red, and gold for the sky, desert, and sun because they represent sunlight, energy, and the Egyptian setting. The lower part uses darker colors such as black, gray, and deep red to represent the Duat, danger, and Apophis. The contrast between the two areas helps show Ra leaving the light and entering darkness.

SHAPE

Simple but recognizable

We used strong geometric shapes throughout the artwork, especially in the pyramids, sun, desert, and Ra's clothing. The circular sun immediately draws attention to Ra and represents the solar cycle. The sharp and irregular shape of Apophis is different from the cleaner shapes above, helping him appear more threatening and chaotic.

MOVEMENT

Following Ra's journey

The artwork guides the viewer's eyes from the bright sun and pyramids toward Ra in the center, then downward toward the boat and Apophis. Ra's position and the direction of the boat create the feeling that he is continuing forward into the Duat. Apophis stretching across the foreground also makes it feel like Ra's path is being blocked.

SYMBOLISM

The story through symbols

The sun disk represents Ra, sunlight, life, and rebirth. The solar boat represents his journey through the Duat every night. Apophis represents chaos, darkness, and the forces trying to stop Ra from completing his journey. The pyramids and desert establish the Egyptian setting, while the contrast between the bright sky and dark foreground represents the continuing struggle between light and darkness, order and chaos.
